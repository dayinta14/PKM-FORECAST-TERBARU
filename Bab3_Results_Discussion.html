The price-level heterogeneity across the four tickers is immediately apparent. ITMG trades at a mean price roughly fifteen times that of ADRO (IDR 6,904.77 versus IDR 452.53), and its standard deviation (IDR 5,627.51) is more than sixteen times that of ADRO. INDY exhibits the highest coefficient of variation relative to its mean among the mid-range stocks, indicating substantial relative volatility. The distributional properties of daily log returns further corroborate this heterogeneity. All four return distributions are strongly leptokurtic (kurtosis ranging from 9.60 for PTBA to 18.31 for ITMG), and the Jarque-Bera test rejects normality at the 1% level for every ticker ($p < 0.001$). INDY and ITMG display pronounced positive skewness (1.00 and 0.92, respectively), reflecting occasional sharp upward price spikes — a feature consistent with commodity-linked equities that respond to supply-side disruptions. By contrast, ADRO and PTBA exhibit slight negative skewness ($-0.05$ and $-0.21$), indicating marginally greater downside risk. INDY is the only stock with a negative mean daily return ($-0.0043$%), suggesting a secular price decline across the twenty-year sample that distinguishes it from the other three equities. These distributional asymmetries confirm that each series possesses a distinct data-generating process, reinforcing the rationale for independent modelling.

The universal I(1) classification validates the use of $d = 1$ in the ARIMA$(p,1,q)$ specification for all four equities, consistent with the standard Box-Jenkins methodology for non-stationary financial time series.

The selected ARIMA configurations vary across tickers, reflecting the heterogeneous autocorrelation structures in each price series. ADRO is best described by a pure moving-average model of order three, ARIMA$(0,1,3)$, indicating that price changes are predominantly driven by short-term shock propagation rather than autoregressive persistence. In contrast, PTBA and INDY both require ARIMA$(3,1,3)$, incorporating both third-order autoregressive and moving-average terms — a specification that captures more complex linear dependencies, consistent with the higher volatility and richer temporal dynamics observed in these series. The diversity in the autoregressive order ($p \in \{0,1,3\}$) underscores the importance of the stock-specific modelling approach: a single pooled ARIMA order would necessarily compromise the fit for one or more tickers.

For ITMG, an additional pattern emerges: the standalone ARIMA (MAPE 15.47%) outperforms the standalone LSTM (MAPE 22.40%) in terms of percentage error, while the hybrid further improves upon both. This suggests that the linear component in ITMG's price dynamics is relatively strong — possibly reflecting the influence of stable institutional demand and dividend patterns for this blue-chip coal stock — and that the residual nonlinear component is modest but still measurable.

The negative $R^2$ values observed for several model–ticker combinations merit careful interpretation. A negative out-of-sample $R^2$ indicates that the model's predictions are worse than a constant forecast equal to the test-set mean. This phenomenon is common in financial forecasting, particularly when the test period (2023–2024) exhibits distributional characteristics that diverge from the training period (2004–2022) — a condition known as dataset shift or concept drift. The 2023–2024 test period coincides with a post-commodity-supercycle correction phase following the extraordinary coal-price rally of 2021–2022, which may explain the deterioration in model performance relative to training-set metrics.

3.5 Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM versus LSTM Performance Analysis

The comparison between the Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM and standalone LSTM merits a dedicated analysis, as the results defy the common expectation that a hybrid model should always outperform its individual components. Drawing from both the out-of-sample test results (Table 8) and the expanding-window validation (Table 9), the following patterns and explanations emerge.

Conditions Favouring the Hybrid Model

On the 2023–2024 test set, the Hybrid ARIMA-LSTM outperforms the standalone LSTM for ADRO (MAPE 11.97% vs. 24.48%), PTBA (5.29% vs. 11.74%), and ITMG (8.51% vs. 22.40%). In these cases, the ARIMA component provides a reasonable baseline linear forecast, and the LSTM residual correction acts as a targeted refinement that reduces the remaining prediction error. The additive decomposition $\hat{Y}_t = \hat{L}_t + \hat{\hat{\epsilon}}_t$ is effective because the linear component captures a meaningful proportion of the price dynamics — autoregressive momentum, trend persistence, and mean-reverting behaviour — leaving a residual series with lower variance and more tractable nonlinear patterns for the LSTM to learn.

PTBA exemplifies this scenario. Its ARIMA component produces a comparatively low standalone MAPE of 9.61%, indicating that the linear structure in PTBA's price series is relatively strong. The LSTM residual correction then reduces this to 5.29%, achieving the best overall performance among all stock–model combinations. The complementarity between the linear and nonlinear components is maximised when both contribute meaningfully and neither dominates the combined forecast with excessive error.

Conditions Favouring the Standalone LSTM

INDY presents the clearest case of standalone LSTM superiority, with a MAPE of 4.32% on the test set compared to 29.94% for the Hybrid. This pattern is consistent across all three cross-validation folds, confirming that it is not an artefact of the test-period selection. Several factors contribute to this outcome:

  1. Error propagation from the ARIMA component. The standalone ARIMA produces an exceptionally poor forecast for INDY (MAPE 54.44%, $R^2 = -7.697$), indicating that the linear approximation fails catastrophically for this ticker. In the hybrid framework, the ARIMA forecast $\hat{L}_t$ constitutes the base prediction, and the LSTM correction $\hat{\hat{\epsilon}}_t$ must compensate for both the inherent nonlinear patterns and the large systematic error introduced by ARIMA. When the base error is sufficiently large, the LSTM residual model — which is trained on in-sample residuals that may not reflect the magnitude of out-of-sample ARIMA failures — cannot adequately correct the distorted baseline.
  2. Dominance of nonlinear dynamics. INDY exhibits the highest return volatility (standard deviation 3.09%), the strongest positive skewness (1.00), and a negative mean daily return ($-0.0043$%). This distributional profile suggests that INDY's price dynamics are governed primarily by nonlinear processes — including volatility clustering, regime-dependent mean levels, and jump-like behaviour — that resist linear decomposition. The end-to-end LSTM, operating directly on the price series, can learn these nonlinear patterns holistically without the intermediation of a misspecified linear component.
  3. Structural changes in the data-generating process. INDY has undergone multiple corporate actions and ownership transitions during the sample period, which may have altered the statistical properties of the price series in ways that violate the stationarity assumptions underlying ARIMA. The LSTM's non-parametric, data-driven architecture is inherently more robust to such structural breaks.

A broadly similar, though less extreme, pattern is observed in the expanding-window results for ITMG, where the LSTM consistently achieves lower MAPE than the Hybrid across all three folds (e.g., Fold 3: LSTM 16.57% vs. Hybrid 41.91%). This further suggests that the hybrid advantage observed on the 2023–2024 test set for ITMG may be period-specific rather than structurally robust.

Implications for Hybrid Model Design

These findings lead to a nuanced conclusion: the hybrid ARIMA-LSTM framework is most effective when the linear component provides a competent — though imperfect — baseline forecast, leaving a manageable residual for the LSTM to model. When the ARIMA component produces large systematic errors, the hybrid architecture can underperform a standalone LSTM that learns the full nonlinear mapping without linear intermediation. Future research may consider adaptive weighting mechanisms or model-selection switches that dynamically evaluate the quality of the ARIMA component before incorporating it into the hybrid combination.

Researchers and practitioners should treat these scenarios as boundary-condition exercises rather than actionable price targets.

3.8 Discussion: External Factors, Model Limitations, and Future Directions

The forecasting framework developed in this study is a univariate time-series model that relies exclusively on the historical adjusted closing price of each equity. While this design enables methodological clarity and reproducibility, it necessarily excludes exogenous factors that demonstrably influence coal-stock prices. A comprehensive discussion of these limitations contextualises the empirical results and motivates future extensions.

Coal Price Shocks and Global Commodity Dynamics

Indonesian coal equities are tightly coupled to the international thermal coal benchmark, particularly the Newcastle Coal Index (ICE Newcastle). Exogenous shocks to global coal markets — including supply disruptions (e.g., flooding in Australian mines), demand surges (e.g., Chinese import policy reversals), and geopolitical events (e.g., the 2022 Russia-Ukraine conflict that redirected European energy procurement) — propagate directly into IDX coal-stock prices. The 2021–2022 commodity supercycle, during which Newcastle coal prices exceeded USD 400/tonne, drove extraordinary price increases across all four tickers, followed by a sharp correction in 2023. This sequence constitutes a classic regime change that violates the distributional stationarity assumed by ARIMA and challenges the training-set distributional assumptions of the LSTM. The elevated MAPE values observed in Fold 3 of the expanding-window validation (validation year 2022) directly reflect this phenomenon.

Energy Transition Risk

The global transition from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources introduces a long-horizon structural risk to coal-sector equities that is absent from the historical training data. Policy developments such as carbon pricing mechanisms, coal phase-out commitments under the Paris Agreement, and Indonesia's own net-zero emissions target for 2060 may fundamentally alter the demand trajectory for thermal coal, rendering historical price patterns an unreliable guide to future dynamics. This structural break risk is particularly relevant to the 2025–2045 scenario projections (Section 3.6), which assume structural continuity that is inconsistent with the expected trajectory of global energy policy.

Corporate Actions and Idiosyncratic Events

Stock splits, rights issues, dividend distributions, and changes in corporate ownership can introduce discontinuities in the adjusted closing price series that are not attributable to market-wide or sector-level dynamics. INDY, in particular, has undergone ownership transitions and corporate restructuring during the sample period, which may partly explain the distinctive statistical properties (negative mean return, high skewness) that set it apart from the other three tickers. The univariate model treats all price movements as endogenous, which may lead to misattribution of corporate-action-driven price changes as learnable patterns.

Market Regime Changes

The Indonesian equity market has experienced multiple regime transitions during the 2004–2024 sample period, including the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, the 2015–2016 commodity downturn, the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic shock, and the 2021–2022 commodity supercycle. Each regime is characterised by distinct volatility levels, trend directions, and correlation structures. A univariate model that pools all regimes into a single training set implicitly averages across these heterogeneous conditions, potentially diluting the model's ability to adapt to any single regime. The expanding-window validation results demonstrate this sensitivity: model performance varies substantially across validation years, with Fold 3 (2022) consistently producing the highest errors. Future work could incorporate regime-detection mechanisms — such as Markov-switching models or structural break tests — to modulate the forecasting strategy conditionally on the identified market state.

Limitations of Univariate Modelling

The exclusive reliance on adjusted closing price as the sole input variable represents a deliberate methodological constraint that prioritises parsimony and reproducibility. However, this constraint excludes potentially informative features such as trading volume, bid-ask spread, macroeconomic indicators (GDP growth, interest rates, exchange rates), sector-level fundamentals (coal production volumes, export quotas, reserve estimates), and alternative data sources (news sentiment, satellite imagery of coal stockpiles). The incorporation of exogenous variables through multivariate extensions — such as ARIMAX, LSTM with exogenous inputs, or attention-based Transformer architectures — constitutes a natural direction for future research that may improve forecast accuracy, particularly over longer horizons where univariate models exhibit rapid uncertainty accumulation.
